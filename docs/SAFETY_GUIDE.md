# Staying safe with astrology / "advice" apps

Applies to this app and to any similar consumer app that asks for birth details, chats and payments.

## Before you install
- Read the **Data safety** section on the Play Store page, then compare with what the app asks for.
- Prefer using the website or a browser tab when an app isn't needed.

## Permissions and identifiers
- **Reset your Advertising ID**: Settings -> Privacy -> Ads -> *Delete / reset advertising ID* (or opt out of personalisation). Do this every few months.
- Deny optional permissions (notifications full-screen, microphone/camera until a call). Check *Settings -> Privacy -> Permission manager* for unused grants.
- Use *Settings -> Apps -> (app) -> Permissions -> Remove permissions if app isn't used*.
- A separate Android user profile or work profile keeps these apps away from the rest of your phone's data.

## What you type
- Treat birth date/time/place, partner details and chat text as **personal data**. Use a nickname and approximate details if you can.
- Don't discuss health, money problems, other people's secrets or legal trouble in chat.
- Don't share OTPs, banking details, or photo IDs in a consultation.

## Money
- Use a payment method with a limit (virtual card). **Turn off auto-pay / auto top-up** and don't save cards.
- Check wallet balance, offers and "free" minutes terms before starting a session; screenshot what you were promised.

## Privacy rights
- Use the app's data-deletion/account-deletion option; then email the company asking for deletion and a list of third parties that received your data (India: DPDP Act 2023 rights; EU/UK: GDPR). Keep the reply.
- Check the privacy policy for retention, sharing with advertisers/analytics, and who can see recordings.

## Network hygiene
- Avoid unknown Wi-Fi when using apps that load content over plain HTTP.
- A private-DNS / tracker-blocking setting (e.g. Android *Private DNS* with a filtering resolver) reduces analytics calls, though it won't stop first-party collection.

## Check any app yourself
Install **PCAPdroid** (TLS decryption off), select the app, use it for a few minutes, and look at *which hosts it contacts at launch*. Many hosts you don't recognise, contacts before you tap anything, or plain-HTTP traffic are worth a closer look.

## If you think an app misused your data
Document it (dates, screenshots), contact the company's grievance officer, then your national regulator (India: Data Protection Board / consumer helpline 1915; EU/UK: your data-protection authority).
